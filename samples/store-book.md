# Learning That Lasts

*A short field guide to studying less and remembering more.*

## Chapter 1: Why We Forget

Memory is not a recording. Every time you recall something, your brain rebuilds it from fragments, and every rebuild is a chance to make it stronger or let it fade. Within a day of a lecture, most people have lost more than half of what they heard.

Forgetting is not a failure of effort. It is the brain clearing space for what seems to matter. The trick is to show it, again and again, that this matters.

- New information fades fastest in the first 24 hours.
- Each successful recall slows the next round of forgetting.
- Information linked to something you already know lasts longer.

## Chapter 2: Retrieval Practice

Rereading feels productive because the words look familiar, but familiarity is not memory. Testing yourself is harder, and that difficulty is exactly what makes it work.

Close the book and write down everything you remember. Then open it and check. The gaps you find are the parts worth studying next.

| Method | Effort | What you remember a week later |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rereading | Low | About a third |
| Highlighting only | Low | About a third |
| Self-testing | High | More than half |
| Teaching someone else | High | The most |

## Chapter 3: Spacing It Out

Ten hours of study spread across two weeks beats ten hours in one night. Each gap lets a little forgetting happen, and recalling something you have half forgotten is what fixes it in place.

A simple schedule works well: review new material the next day, then after three days, then after a week, then after a month.

1. Learn it today.
2. Recall it tomorrow.
3. Recall it again in three days.
4. Recall it again next week.

## Chapter 4: Mixing Topics

Practising one kind of problem until you master it, then moving to the next, feels efficient. Mixing different kinds of problem in one session feels slower and messier, yet it teaches you to recognise which approach each problem needs.

Interleaving works for maths, languages, music and sport alike. The moment of choosing is the skill.

## Chapter 5: Making It Your Own

Explain each idea in your own words, as if to a friend. Draw it. Connect it to an example from your own life. The more ways an idea is stored, the more routes you have back to it.

Questions such as "why does this work?" and "how is this different from that?" turn a fact into understanding.

## Chapter 6: Rest and Sleep

Sleep is when the day's learning is sorted and stored. A night of good sleep after studying protects what you learned; a short night undoes much of it.

Short breaks help too. A ten-minute walk between study sessions lets the brain replay what it has just taken in.
